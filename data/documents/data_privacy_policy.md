## 1. Purpose

This policy governs the collection, use, access, retention, and disclosure of personal data and Restricted data at Northstar Analytics. It defines practical controls for ordinary work and escalation when a privacy concern occurs.

## 2. Scope

It applies to data about employees, applicants, customers, users, and business contacts when Northstar processes it. Project-specific instructions may impose additional restrictions.

## 3. Definitions

- **Personal data** means information that identifies or can reasonably be linked to a person.
- **Restricted data** means data labeled Restricted under IS-002, including personal data requiring heightened controls.
- **Processing** means collecting, viewing, using, changing, sharing, storing, or deleting data.
- **Privacy incident** means suspected unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, alteration, or collection of personal data.

## 4. Policy Rules

### DP-001 — Purpose Limitation

Employees may process personal data only for an approved business purpose and only to the extent needed for that purpose. Employees must not collect additional personal data merely because it might be useful later.

### DP-002 — Access Authorization

Access to personal or Restricted data must be approved by the data owner or delegated approver and limited to the employee's work need. Employees must not browse records out of curiosity.

### DP-003 — Restricted Data Controls

Restricted data must be stored and transmitted using controls approved for its classification under IS-003 and IS-008. Employees must not send Restricted data through a personal account or unapproved public service.

### DP-004 — Disclosure

Employees must not disclose personal data to an external recipient unless the disclosure has an approved business purpose and the Privacy Office or authorized process permits it. The recipient and disclosed data must be recorded when required by the process.

### DP-005 — Retention

Employees must retain personal data only for the approved retention period. When the period ends, the data owner must arrange secure deletion or archival under the applicable records schedule.

### DP-006 — Travel and Cross-Border Handling

Employees handling Restricted data while traveling must use an approved device and approved storage. Cross-border processing requires Privacy Office approval before the processing begins, unless an approved project instruction already covers the location.

### DP-007 — Data Subject Requests

Employees who receive a request to access, correct, or delete personal data must forward it to the Privacy Office within 2 business days and must not answer independently.

### DP-008 — Privacy Incident Escalation

Suspected unauthorized access, loss, disclosure, or collection of personal data must be reported under IR-002 within 24 hours of discovery. Employees must preserve relevant evidence and must not notify an affected person unless directed.

## 5. Exceptions

The Privacy Office may approve a documented exception for a defined project, legal hold, safety need, or operational emergency. The exception must specify data, purpose, location, controls, owner, and expiration. An exception does not override a security incident reporting deadline.

## 6. Procedures

The employee checks classification and purpose before processing. The data owner approves access and retention. Employees route requests to Privacy and incidents to the incident channel. The Privacy Office records approvals, reviews cross-border activity, and coordinates required response.

## 7. Responsibilities

Privacy decisions depend on the purpose, data category, recipient, and location involved. Employees should stop and seek guidance when any of those facts are unclear instead of making a broad disclosure or retaining extra data. The Privacy Office may coordinate with Security, Legal, People Operations, or a project owner, but coordination does not transfer the employee's duty to report a suspected incident promptly.

Employees use data lawfully within assigned work and report concerns. Managers sponsor business need. Data owners approve access and retention. The Privacy Office owns interpretations, cross-border approvals, requests, and privacy incident coordination.
