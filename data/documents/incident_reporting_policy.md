## 1. Purpose

This policy defines what must be reported to Northstar Analytics, how quickly reports must be made, and how incidents are triaged and escalated. It creates a common intake path while preserving specialized response ownership.

## 2. Scope

It applies to security, privacy, safety, conduct, operational, and availability events connected to Northstar work or systems. A report may be made even when facts are incomplete or the final severity is uncertain.

## 3. Definitions

- **Incident** means a suspected or confirmed event that may harm people, information, systems, operations, compliance, or Northstar's reputation.
- **Near miss** means an event that could have caused harm but did not.
- **Severity** means the initial impact category assigned during triage.
- **Discovery** means when a person first reasonably becomes aware of the suspected event.

## 4. Policy Rules

### IR-001 — Reportable Events

Employees must report suspected security incidents, privacy incidents, workplace injuries, near misses, material conduct concerns, unauthorized disclosure, and significant service disruption. Uncertainty about final impact is not a reason to delay a report.

### IR-002 — Initial Reporting Deadline

An employee must submit an initial incident report within 24 hours of discovery. If the reporting channel is unavailable, the employee must notify the manager or service desk and submit the report when the channel is restored.

### IR-003 — Required Report Information

The initial report must include the reporter's name and contact, discovery time, affected system or location, observed facts, people or data potentially affected, immediate actions taken, and supporting evidence when available.

### IR-004 — Severity Triage

Risk and Response assigns an initial severity: Critical for immediate threat to life, widespread Restricted-data exposure, or major business interruption; High for material but contained impact; Medium for limited impact requiring coordinated action; and Low for a minor event requiring tracking.

### IR-005 — Escalation

Critical incidents must be escalated to the incident commander immediately and to the executive duty contact within 1 hour. High incidents must be escalated to the incident commander within 4 hours. Medium and Low incidents must be routed to the responsible owner for review.

### IR-006 — Preservation and Confidentiality

Reporters must preserve relevant messages, files, device information, and observations when safe to do so. Employees must not delete evidence, speculate publicly, or disclose incident details outside the response group.

### IR-007 — Specialized Routing

Security events must be routed to Information Security, privacy events to the Privacy Office, safety events to Workplace Operations, and conduct concerns to Ethics and Compliance. Routing does not remove the initial 24-hour reporting duty.

### IR-008 — Status Updates

The incident commander must record material status updates and assigned actions. Employees involved in response must provide requested facts promptly and must not independently contact affected external parties unless authorized.

## 5. Exceptions

A person facing an immediate safety threat should seek emergency assistance before reporting. A person without access to the reporting channel may use the manager or service desk as an alternate initial report. These exceptions change the channel or sequence, not the duty to report.

## 6. Procedures

The reporter makes the person or area safe, records known facts, submits the report, and preserves evidence. Risk and Response acknowledges and triages it, assigns an owner, sets severity, and coordinates updates. The specialized owner investigates and records closure evidence.

## 7. Responsibilities

The initial report is a factual intake, not a completed investigation. Reporters should distinguish what they observed from what they suspect and should identify missing information. Risk and Response may revise severity as evidence develops. Response participants must use the assigned channel for updates so that decisions, owners, and timing remain traceable. An employee who is unsure whether an event meets a category should report it and allow triage to decide.

Reporters provide timely factual information. Managers help employees report and escalate urgent issues. Risk and Response owns triage and coordination. Specialized owners investigate their incident types. The incident commander directs Critical and High response.
